    Why bones weaken in space — and what helps

    What happens

    Without gravity, bones carry no load. Cells that break bone down speed up, while bone-building cells don’t keep pace. Hip and spine lose about 1–1.5% per month — faster than an elderly person loses in a year.

    Load it heavy

    Heavy resistance exercise (squats, deadlifts, heel raises at ≥100% of body weight) plus treadmill running protects most sites. About 1.5 h of total exercise a day, 6–7 days a week, is the ISS routine.

    Fuel it

    Eat enough calories, get enough protein and vitamin D, and keep sodium ≤ 3,000 mg. Weight loss and high salt both speed up bone loss.

    Watch your kidneys

    Calcium from bone ends up in urine and can form stones. Drink ≥ 2 L a day. Flank pain with blood in urine needs the flight surgeon.

    Medication

    Exercise alone doesn’t stop loss of the hip’s spongy inner bone. Exercise plus a bisphosphonate (weekly alendronate or a pre-flight zoledronic acid infusion) prevented loss in ISS studies. Only your flight surgeon can prescribe it.

    After landing

    Recovery is slow and often incomplete — about half of astronauts had not recovered within a year. Keep loading, follow the walking-to-jogging plan, and get follow-up scans.
