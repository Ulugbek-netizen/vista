# Clinical reference ranges, action thresholds and self-measurement technique

Compiled 2026-11-14 for Void Voyagers. Web access was available. Journal abstracts and bibliographic data were retrieved from PubMed (DOI links given for each); guideline pages were opened directly unless marked otherwise.
Confidence key: **high** = number read in an opened primary or issuing-body source; **medium** = read in a secondary source or search summary, or recalled from a paper whose DOI was verified; **low** = from memory only.
Band key: Void Voyagers colours are **Nominal / Watch (self-care and re-measure) / Act (tell the crew medical officer or flight surgeon the same day) / Urgent (act immediately)**. Bands marked **(D)** are design proposals because no guideline defines a cut-off; they need flight-surgeon sign-off.

## Thresholds

How the bands were built: where NEWS2 covers an indicator, its per-parameter score maps to the colour (0 = Nominal, 1 = Watch, 2 = Act, 3 = Urgent), then the band is cross-checked against the issuing guideline for that indicator. A single abnormal reading should be repeated once after the correct preparation before it changes colour, except when a red-flag symptom is present.

Red flags that upgrade any reading to Urgent, with their sources: chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, vision change or difficulty speaking with high blood pressure (AHA/ACC via PCNA); cold sweaty skin, rapid breathing, bluish skin or a weak rapid pulse (NHLBI); blue lips, face or nails, chest tightness or a racing pulse with a low SpO2 (FDA); confusion, stiff neck, very bad headache, seizure or being hard to wake with fever (MedlinePlus); altered mental state or inability to swallow with low glucose (ADA); any new distortion or blank area on the Amsler grid needs at least a same-day report (AAO).

## Measurement in space

- **Resting heart rate.** Device: manual wrist pulse, or a wearable ECG such as the CSA Bio-Monitor shirt (heart rate and ECG, blood pressure, breathing rate and volume, skin temperature, SpO2 and activity; flown to the ISS in December 2018). Steps: (1) rest for 10 minutes; (2) place index and middle fingers on the wrist below the base of the thumb; (3) count for 60 seconds, or 30 seconds × 2; (4) note whether the rhythm is regular; (5) log with the time. Cadence: every morning on waking, and whenever symptomatic.
- **Blood pressure.** Device: validated upper-arm cuff (the 2025 guideline does not yet recommend cuffless devices for readings). Steps: (1) no food, drink, caffeine or exercise for 30 minutes and empty the bladder; (2) rest still for 5 minutes, restrained and relaxed in place of sitting; (3) cuff on the bare upper arm at chest level; (4) do not talk; (5) take 2 readings 1–2 minutes apart and log both. Cadence: weekly at the same time of day; daily for 7 days after any Watch reading (D).
- **SpO2.** Device: fingertip pulse oximeter. Steps: (1) warm, relaxed hand; (2) remove nail polish; (3) stay still; (4) wait until the number is steady; (5) log the value with date and time. Cadence: weekly spot check, plus with any breathlessness or after a cabin atmosphere alarm (D).
- **Respiratory rate.** Device: observation, or a wearable such as Bio-Monitor. Steps (standard practice, not taken from an opened source): (1) rest for 5 minutes; (2) count chest rises for a full 60 seconds; (3) do not talk; (4) have a crewmate count if possible, because awareness changes breathing; (5) log. Cadence: with each heart-rate check when unwell.
- **Body temperature.** Device: digital oral thermometer or tympanic thermometer; the ISS core-temperature study used a non-invasive forehead sensor (recalled from the paper, not stated in its abstract). Steps: (1) wait 20–30 minutes after eating or drinking and 1 hour after heavy exercise; (2) place the probe under the tongue, lips closed, breathing through the nose; (3) hold until the beep (up to 3 minutes); (4) always use the same site, because the ear reads 0.3–0.6 °C higher than the mouth; (5) log value, site and time. Cadence: daily at a fixed time for the first weeks to set the in-flight baseline, then when unwell.
- **Capillary glucose.** Device: finger-stick glucose meter (routine in-flight use on the ISS was not confirmed). Steps (standard practice, not taken from an opened source; follow the meter's instructions): (1) clean and dry the fingertip; (2) insert a strip; (3) lance the side of the fingertip; (4) apply the drop; (5) log value, time and time since last meal. Cadence: not routine for crew without diabetes; fasting check when symptomatic or on flight-surgeon request (D).
- **Sleep.** Device: wrist actigraphy plus a daily sleep log, the method used in the ISS sleep studies. Steps: (1) wear the device continuously; (2) each morning log lights-out and wake times; (3) log awakenings and sleep quality; (4) log any sleep medication; (5) review the 7-day average. Cadence: daily.
- **Body mass.** Device: NASA SLAMMD (acceleration, F = ma) or the Russian BMMD (spring oscillation). Steps: (1) always use the same device; (2) follow the device procedure and stay rigid; (3) take repeated measures and average; (4) same time of day and clothing; (5) log as % change from the pre-flight scale mass. Cadence: weekly in the first 30 days, then monthly, weekly again after any Watch result (D).
- **Hydration.** Device: fluid-intake log, 8-point urine colour chart, urine volume estimate. Steps: (1) log every drink and rehydrated food pouch; (2) judge urine colour in a clear container against the chart under white light, and note whether it is the first-morning void, which normally reads darker (corrected); (3) record the chart number; (4) note the number of voids; (5) compare intake with the personal requirement of 32 mL per kg body mass, at least 2.5 L for men or 2.1 L for women (corrected). Cadence: daily.
- **Mood and stress.** Device: 0–10 self-ratings in the app, plus the PHQ-4. Steps: (1) rate mood and stress at the same time each day; (2) complete the PHQ-4 every 2 weeks; (3) answer a separate direct question about thoughts of self-harm; (4) add a free-text note; (5) the app compares with the personal 14-day average. Cadence: daily ratings, PHQ-4 every 2 weeks; the ISS Reaction Self-Test collected stress and workload ratings every 4 days.
- **Psychomotor vigilance.** Device: the ISS Reaction Self-Test, a Windows-based PVT-B. Steps: (1) use the same device and input method every time; (2) restrain yourself and remove distractions; (3) respond as fast as possible without anticipating; (4) run the full 3 minutes; (5) log response speed, lapses (355 ms or more) and false starts (below 100 ms). Cadence: every 4 days as on the ISS, plus before safety-critical tasks and after any night under 6 h (D).
- **Near visual acuity and Amsler grid.** Device: near acuity card and Amsler grid; in flight NASA also uses an ocular questionnaire, fundoscopy, ultrasound and OCT. Steps for the grid: (1) wear usual reading glasses; (2) hold it 12–15 inches (30–38 cm) away in good light; (3) cover one eye; (4) fix on the centre dot and check for wavy, blurry, dark or blank areas; (5) repeat with the other eye. For near acuity, hold the card at its calibrated distance (typically 14–16 inches, 36–40 cm), test one eye at a time and record the smallest line read. Cadence: weekly self-check (D), in addition to NASA's schedule of flight day 30, flight day 90 and 30 days before return (corrected: "every 90 days" removed).
- **Hand-grip strength.** Device: hand dynamometer (a specific ISS model was not confirmed). Steps (protocol details recalled from Roberts et al. 2011, abstract re-read only): (1) same body position each time, elbow at 90°, wrist neutral; (2) squeeze as hard as possible for 3–5 seconds; (3) 3 trials per hand, alternating hands; (4) rest between trials; (5) log the best value per hand. Cadence: every 2–4 weeks (D).
- **Pain.** Device: 0–10 numerical scale. Steps: (1) rate current pain; (2) rate the worst pain in the last 24 hours; (3) record site and onset; (4) record what makes it better or worse; (5) flag any red-flag symptom. Cadence: daily prompt, and at onset.
- **HRV (RMSSD).** Device: ECG-based wearable (chest strap or sensor shirt) or optical ring. Steps: (1) measure on waking; (2) same posture each time; (3) record for at least 1 minute, 5 minutes if possible; (4) discard recordings with movement artefact; (5) track the 7-day rolling mean against the personal baseline. Cadence: daily.

## Caveats

- **Not a validated clinical tool.** Every row marked (D) is a design proposal. No guideline defines Watch/Act/Urgent bands for healthy astronauts, and NEWS2, ADA, AHA/ACC and GLIM thresholds were all built for terrestrial patients. A flight surgeon must approve the bands before crew use.
- **AHA primary pages were not readable.** heart.org returned HTTP 403 and the journal site blocks automated access, so the severe-hypertension and hypertensive-emergency wording comes from PCNA and AMA (both co-issuing organisations) and a search summary. The exact AHA instruction on how long to wait before re-measuring a reading above 180/120 mmHg was not confirmed.
- **NEWS2 versus healthy-adult ranges disagree at the low end.** NEWS2 scores systolic 101–110 mmHg as 1 and 91–100 as 2, and pulse 41–50 as 1, while MedlinePlus lists 90/60–120/80 mmHg and (for athletes) 40–60 beats/min as normal. NEWS2 also scores 12–20 breaths/min as normal where MedlinePlus says 12–18. The table resolves this with the personal baseline; fit crew will otherwise trigger false Watch alerts.
- **NEWS2 may not be altered.** The RCP allows free reuse only if the material is unmodified, so the app must either compute true NEWS2 (which also needs consciousness and supplemental-oxygen inputs) with the RCP citation, or describe its bands as "informed by" NEWS2. NEWS2 is designed for acutely ill adults in care settings; its exclusions (commonly stated as under 16 and pregnancy) were not re-verified here.
- **ADA hypoglycaemia levels assume diabetes.** In people without diabetes a capillary value of 60–70 mg/dL after fasting can be physiological; the conventional symptomatic threshold of about 55 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L) is from memory and was not verified. ADA thresholds are for plasma glucose; consumer meters carry an error of roughly ±15% (from memory, not verified), and ADA does not endorse CGM for diagnosis.
- **Fever cut-offs differ by source and site.** CDC uses 38.0 °C, MedlinePlus 37.2–37.5 °C oral, and NEWS2 does not score until 38.1 °C (corrected: the "rise of 0.6–0.8 °C above personal normal" previously attributed to MedlinePlus is not on that page). Ear readings run 0.3–0.6 °C above oral. The 1 °C in-flight rise was measured as core temperature with a research sensor, not with an oral or ear thermometer.
- **Pain cut-points.** The validated cut-points (1–4, 5–6, 7–10) come from cancer-pain data; the commonly quoted 1–3, 4–6, 7–10 split was not found in a primary source. Other populations give different boundaries.
- **PVT.** No typical mean reaction time or absolute impairment cut-off could be sourced. From memory, rested adults average roughly 250–300 ms on the 10-minute PVT, but the developers advise against using mean reaction time at all. The 355 ms threshold was derived post hoc on one device, so Void Voyagers timings on a browser or touchscreen are not comparable with published values and need their own baseline.
- **Urine colour.** The original 1994 paper's abstract gives no cut-offs and says colour should not replace laboratory measures. The cut-offs used come from later studies (one in young men exercising in heat, one in a mixed adult sample). Licence terms for reproducing the Armstrong chart artwork were not checked; the team should draw its own colour scale.
- **GLIM and ICSD-3 numbers** were confirmed only in secondary summaries; the GLIM abstract and the official ICSD-3 text were not read. The EWGSOP2 grip cut-offs (27 kg and 16 kg) are recalled from the paper and apply to older adults; healthy crew sit far above them, so the personal-baseline band matters more.
- **EFSA figures** (2.5 and 2.0 L/day total water, which includes water in food) are recalled; the page redirected and was not read. The NASA 2.5 L figure is from the current web edition of Volume 2, whose revision letter is not shown; older revisions quoted 2.0 kg for drinking only.
- **SANS numbers.** The NASA brief quotes hyperopic shifts of +0.5 to +2.0 D whereas the Mader 2011 abstract gives +0.50 to +1.75 D; the abstract's figure is used. The near-acuity "lines lost" bands and the ophthalmic red-flag list are clinical convention, not from an opened source.
- **PHQ-4 has no suicide item,** its severity bands were read on a secondary rehabilitation-measures site, and the GAD-2 sensitivity and specificity (86% and 83%) come from secondary citations of Kroenke 2007. No validated cut-off exists for a single 0–10 mood item.
- **ISS hardware not confirmed:** the specific cuff, thermometer, pulse oximeter, glucose meter and grip dynamometer in the ISS medical kit; NASA's scheduled sleep duration; any in-flight grip-strength norms; WHO or BTS oximetry thresholds; the orthostatic hypotension definition.
- **Microgravity adaptations of technique** (staying restrained instead of seated for blood pressure, judging urine colour in a clear container) are inferences, not sourced procedures.

### Conflicts with the behaviour-sleep-immune file that the team must resolve

1. **Body temperature 38.0 to 39.3 °C with no symptoms**: Act here, Watch in the behaviour-sleep-immune file. This file makes any reading of 40.6 °C or more Urgent; the other file uses 40.0 °C. Pick one rule set before coding.
2. **Meaning of "Urgent"**: here it means "act immediately" and a PHQ-4 total of 9 to 12 is Act; the other file calls a same-day private conference Urgent and applies it to 9 to 12.
3. **Stress rating of 7 or more**: Act here; the other file does not colour stress by absolute value.
4. **PVT-B timing**: 1 to 4 s intervals here (2011 handheld version); the flight version in the other file uses 2 to 5 s.
5. **Sleep**: the other file's Act band was changed at verification to match this one (2 or more consecutive nights under 6 h, or a 7-day mean under 6 h) and adds "under 5 h on any single night".

### Alarm-fatigue, coverage and edition notes (no value changed)

- **T05 coverage gap**: a first reading of 39.4 to 40.5 °C is in no band until it is repeated. The rule engine should treat it as at least Act while the repeat is pending.
- **T02 coverage gap**: a systolic reading of 90 or less with no symptoms, in someone for whom it is not the usual value, is in no band (Act needs dizziness, Urgent needs shock signs). NEWS2 scores 90 or less as 3; assign it at least Act until a flight surgeon decides otherwise.
- **T08 wording**: the Watch cell mixes a per-night band ("6.0–6.9") with a per-week count ("1–2 nights below 7 in a week"); decide which of the two the rule engine uses.
- **T08**: the group mean of in-flight sleep is 6.09 h (SD 0.67 h), so a large share of crew will meet "7-day average below 6 h". Raise Act once and then track it.
- **T03 and T17**: Watch at an SpO2 of 95% and at a 1-line acuity change will fire on normal measurement noise.
- **CLN-19**: ICSD-3 has been superseded by ICSD-3-TR (2023); the 3-month criterion was confirmed only in a secondary table of the 2014 edition.
- **Editions**: ADA Standards of Care 2026, the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline, NEWS2 (2017), NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1 Rev C and Volume 2 Rev F (approved 2026-07-14) are the current editions as of this check.
